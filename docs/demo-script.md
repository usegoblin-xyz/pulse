# Pulse — Video Demo Script (use-case driven)

For a screen-recorded demo you narrate and drive yourself. Built around Pulse's
real capabilities: live web research **with citations**, **on-demand screen
vision**, and a document that **writes itself in the background while you keep
talking**. Villain-flavored host: Errol Vance.

The spine is one continuous story (Use Case 1). Use Cases 2–4 are swap-ins /
extenders you can drop in depending on the audience.

**Record setup:** Chrome, quiet room, mic close. Have the pages you'll "share"
already open in tabs. Say the **YOU** lines naturally; **[do]** = what you click.

---

## THE HOOK (say to camera, 15s)

> "Every AI assistant makes you type, wait, and copy-paste. This one reads the
> live web, looks at your screen, and builds real work while you just… talk to
> it. His name is Pulse."

**[do]** Click **Start conversation**. He appears.

---

## USE CASE 1 — "Competitor to product spec, without lifting a finger"
**Who it's for:** founders, PMs, anyone who's ever done competitive research.
**Why it's compelling:** it chains all three superpowers into one unbroken
conversation and ends with a downloadable deliverable. This is your spine.

**[do]** Click **Share screen** → your competitor's **pricing page** (pick a real
one your audience knows — a SaaS tool, an app, a service).

**YOU:** "Pulse, look at this page. What is this company charging, and what's
their cheapest paid plan?"
> **PULSE:** reads the actual pricing off the screen and tells you.

**YOU:** "Now search what their users complain about the most. Give me the top
gripes, and tell me where you saw them."
> **PULSE:** searches the live web, comes back with real complaints **and names
> the sources.** The Files box fills with the pages he read.

**YOU:** "Perfect. Build me a product requirements document for a tool that fixes
those exact complaints. Get started — you can keep talking to me while you write
it."
> **PULSE:** "I've started that PRD in the background. Keep chatting with me while
> I work — I'll tell you the moment it's ready to download."
> **[watch]** The spec begins **streaming into the Files box**, live.

**YOU** *(while it's visibly writing — this is the money moment):* "While you're
on that — what's their single strongest feature, the one we'd have to match to
be taken seriously?"
> **PULSE:** answers the question — *without dropping the PRD.* The document keeps
> writing itself the whole time.

> **PULSE** *(a few seconds later, unprompted):* "Your PRD is ready in the files
> box, and you can download it now. Want me to change anything?"

**[do]** Click **Download .md**. Open it on camera: a full spec — problem, users,
features with priorities, requirements, milestones, risks, metrics. Click
**View sources** to show it was grounded in real pages.

**Say to camera:**
> "That was competitive research turned into a shippable spec — and he never
> stopped holding a conversation with me. He was working in the background the
> entire time."

## USE CASE 2 — "Brief me before the meeting" (research + citations)
**Who it's for:** everyone in knowledge work. **The anti-hallucination beat.**

**YOU:** "I have a call in five minutes about [a real, current topic your
audience cares about]. Give me the three things I need to know to sound sharp —
and tell me where each one comes from."
> **PULSE:** current, synthesized answer, **each point attributed to a source.**

**YOU:** "Which of those is the most contested — where do the sources actually
disagree?"
> **PULSE:** names the disagreement instead of pretending there's one clean truth.

**Say to camera:**
> "Current, cited, and honest about what's contested. That's the difference
> between an answer you can repeat in a meeting and one you can't trust."

---

## USE CASE 3 — "Read this so I don't have to" (screen vision on dense text)
**Who it's for:** literally everyone. Great for a broad audience.

**[do]** Share a **dense page** — a terms-of-service, a lease, a pricing table
with fine print, a research abstract.

**YOU:** "Pulse, look at this. In plain English — what am I actually agreeing to,
and what should I watch out for?"
> **PULSE:** translates the jargon and flags the catch.

**YOU** *(scroll to / point at a specific clause):* "What does this part mean for
me specifically?"
> **PULSE:** answers about that exact section.

**Say to camera:**
> "He's reading the same screen you are, and turning legalese into 'here's the
> part that bites you.'"

---

## USE CASE 4 — "Is this legit?" (due diligence in seconds)
**Who it's for:** anyone about to trust a company, offer, or product.

**[do]** Share an unfamiliar company's site (a startup, a supplement, an offer).

**YOU:** "Who is this company, and should I trust them? Go find the red flags."
> **PULSE:** researches and reports what's verifiable — and what's suspicious —
> with sources.

**Say to camera:**
> "Before you hand over a card or sign anything, he does the digging you never
> have time to do."

---

## RECOMMENDED FULL FLOW (~4 minutes)

1. **Hook** (15s)
2. **Use Case 1** — competitor → spec, the full chain (2.5 min) ← the spine
3. **One research beat** — Use Case 2 *or* 4, to show cited web research cold (45s)
4. **Companion Mode** (20s): **[do]** click **Companion Mode** — Pulse pops into a
   floating window with your files right under his face. "Now he follows you over
   every app and tab."
5. **Close** (15s): *"He reads the whole web and cites it. He sees your screen
   when you ask. And he builds real work in the background while you keep moving.
   Most assistants make you wait. Pulse just handles it."*

**Shortest version (90s):** Hook → Use Case 1 only (it contains research + vision
+ background work + a download). That single story is the whole pitch.

---

## Delivery notes
- **Let the background moment breathe.** After you ask for the PRD, deliberately
  ask an unrelated question while it streams. The "he kept talking AND kept
  building" contrast is the thing people remember — don't rush past it.
- **Show the receipts.** Every time he cites, glance at the Files box. Trust is
  the whole differentiator.
- **Speak clearly and close to the mic** — the ASR favors a strong, near voice.
- **Only-when-asked is a feature.** He doesn't narrate your screen unprompted;
  point that out — "he's not watching me, he looks when I ask."
- **Pick pages your audience recognizes.** A competitor they know, a topic they
  care about — specificity is what makes it land.

## The three lines to memorize
1. "Current, and cited — answers you can actually repeat."
2. "Share your screen and he just gets it."
3. "He builds real work in the background while you keep talking to him."
