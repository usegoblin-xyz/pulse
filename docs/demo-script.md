# Pulse — Demo Script

A tight, ~4-minute live demo built around what Pulse actually does today:
live web research with citations, on-demand screen vision, and a PRD that
writes itself in the background while you keep talking. Villain-flavored host,
Errol Vance.

**Setup before you start**
- Open https://pulse-demo.fly.dev on Chrome. Mic on, quiet room.
- Have one tab ready to share (e.g. a product's pricing page, or a page you'll
  ask him to "spec out" — a competitor site works great).
- Say each **YOU** line out loud, naturally. Wait for Pulse to finish before the
  next line. The **[do]** notes are what you click.

---

## Cold open — the reveal (20s)

**[do]** Click **Start conversation**. Let the beam settle; Pulse is on screen.

> **PULSE:** "I'm Pulse. Ask me anything and I'll go read the web and come back
> with the real answer. Or share your screen, and I'll tell you what I see."

**Presenter line (to the room, not to Pulse):**
> "This is Pulse. He used to be a quant researcher named Errol Vance — the most
> technical mind in the valley — until it went wrong. Now he reads the entire
> web so you don't have to. Watch."

*Why it lands: you've set a character, not a chatbot. People lean in.*

---

## Act 1 — The web, handled (60s)

**YOU:** "Pulse, what's the most recent thing James Webb has discovered, and when?"

> **PULSE** *(after a beat — he's searching + reading live)*: gives the actual
> recent finding **and names the source** ("from NASA's own Webb page").
> The **Files box** slides in on the left with the real sources he pulled.

**YOU:** "Where did you get that?"

> **PULSE:** names the source in plain speech. **[do]** Point at the Files box —
> "these are the pages he actually read, live, seconds ago. Not training data."

**The one-liner to say:**
> "Every answer comes with its receipts. He cuts twenty open tabs down to one
> straight answer."

*Why it lands: current + cited = trust. This is the anti-hallucination beat.*

---

## Act 2 — He sees what you see (45s)

**[do]** Click **Share screen** → pick the tab/window you prepared.

**YOU:** "Pulse, take a look at my screen. What am I looking at?"

> **PULSE:** describes the page accurately in a sentence or two — the product,
> the key things on it. On demand, only when asked. No creepy constant narration.

**YOU:** *(point at something specific on the page)* "What does this part do?"

> **PULSE:** answers about that element.

**The one-liner:**
> "He's not guessing from a URL. He's looking at the same pixels you are."

*Why it lands: 'share and he just gets it' is the demo people remember.*

---

## Act 3 — The killer move: he works while you talk (90s)

This is the beat that separates Pulse from every other assistant. He starts a
real deliverable and keeps talking to you while it writes itself.

**YOU:** "Pulse, build me a PRD for a product like the one on my screen."
*(or: "…a PRD for a voice-first grocery app for busy families.")*

> **PULSE:** "I've started building that PRD in the background. Keep chatting with
> me about anything while I work — I'll tell you the moment it's ready to
> download."
> **[watch]** The Files box starts **streaming the document live** — sources
> first, then the spec writing itself, section by section.

**Now — while it writes — keep talking. Ask something totally unrelated:**

**YOU:** "While that's cooking — who won the last Super Bowl?"

> **PULSE:** answers the Super Bowl question, cited, *without dropping the PRD.*
> The document keeps streaming in the Files box the whole time.

**The one-liner (say it while the doc is visibly writing):**
> "He didn't freeze. He didn't make you wait on a spinner. He's holding a
> conversation and building a full product spec at the same time — in the
> background."

> **PULSE** *(a few seconds later, unprompted)*: "Your PRD titled … is ready in
> the files box, and you can download it now. Want me to change anything?"

**[do]** Click **Download .md** in the Files box. Open it — a full spec:
Summary, Problem, Goals, Personas, Key Features with priorities, User Stories,
Requirements, Milestones, Risks, Success Metrics. **[do]** Click **View sources**
to show it was grounded in real pages.

*Why it lands: 'ambient work' — the assistant that keeps going while you move on
— is the future people feel but rarely see. This is the mic-drop.*

---

## Act 4 — He comes with you (30s)

**[do]** Click **Companion Mode.**

> Pulse pops out into a small always-on-top window — his face on top, the Files
> box (your sources and that PRD, still downloadable) right below it.

**The one-liner:**
> "Now he floats over whatever you're doing. Research, screen, and your files
> follow you across every app and tab."

---

## Close — the pitch (15s)

> "That's Pulse. He reads the whole web and cites it. He sees your screen when
> you ask. And he builds real work in the background while you keep moving.
>
> Most assistants make you wait. Pulse just handles it."

**[do]** Click **Stop** to end cleanly.

---

## Backup lines & recovery

- **If a web answer is slow:** "He's reading the actual page, not guessing —
  give him the second."
- **If ASR mishears you:** just repeat the line clearly; the mic favors a clear,
  close voice.
- **If vision hits its free-tier cap:** he'll say his eyes need a moment — say
  "we're on the free tier for the demo, this is where you'd flip on more
  capacity," and move to the PRD act (different capability).
- **Shortest possible version (90s):** Act 1 (research + cite) → Act 3 (PRD in the
  background while you ask one unrelated question) → Download. That's the whole
  story.

## The three sentences to memorize
1. "Every answer comes with its receipts — current, and cited."
2. "Share your screen and he just gets it."
3. "He builds real work in the background while you keep talking to him."
